Who we are

Bringing civic engagement to every classroom.

Youth Civics Network NJ is the New Jersey branch of the national Youth Civics Network. Our mission is to make civics nonpartisan, digestible, and genuinely enjoyable for elementary and middle schoolers.

Everything we do is planned, designed, built and taught by high schoolers. Students run every part of it, from the state board down to each local chapter.

Why we do it

Kids deserve to understand the world they're growing up in.

01

Ready for high school and for life

We want every kid who comes through our program to know how government actually works. That means their town council, the State House and the ballot box. We want them ready for high school and ready to be informed citizens.

02

Nonpartisan, always

We never promote any political party or ideology. Outside observers proofread every lesson for neutrality before it reaches a classroom. We teach how the system works, not what to think about it.

03

Fun beats boring

Kids learn best from people just a few years older than them. Our lessons use games, analogies and hands-on activities, like writing a state law or running a mini Electoral College, so civics feels relevant and not like a textbook.

How it works

One hour a month. Big ideas, made simple.

  1. Find your chapter. Each chapter meets once a month at a local library, school or rec center.
  2. Show up and play. Classes are about an hour long, with a warm-up, a short lesson, and a game or activity.
  3. Come back next month. Each module builds on the last, from the federal government down to your own town.

This term's curriculum (through January)

1

How NJ and the Federal Government Share Power

Think of it like a Little League rulebook. Students learn who makes which rules, then write a state law of their own.

2

New Jersey State Politics

The three branches, what the Governor can (and can't) do, and a team challenge to solve a real town's problem.

3

Local Politics

NJ has 21 counties and 564 municipalities. Students meet school boards and learn how local decisions shape their everyday life.

4

How Elections and Voting Work

Registration, the Electoral College, a Blooket quiz, and a "pizza or pasta" mini Electoral College showdown.

5

Coming soon

A fifth module is in the works. The national curriculum also covers topics like State Agencies and State Legislatures.
